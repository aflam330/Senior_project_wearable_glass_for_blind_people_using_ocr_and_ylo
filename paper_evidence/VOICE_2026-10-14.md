# Voice output rebuilt, and voice commands (14 October 2026)

Code: `savior_glass/utils.py` (`TTSEngine`), `savior_glass/voice_commands.py`, `scripts/speech_check.py`, `scripts/get_speech_models.py`, `scripts/test_tts_queue.py`, `scripts/test_voice_commands.py`.
Result files: `results/voice_commands_synthetic.json`, `results/speech_piper_bn_intelligibility.json`, `results/speech_check_<host>.json`.

## 1. The broken voice on the Pi

Reported after the run of 13 October: the voice comes out broken. The sound itself could not be heard from here, so the causes below are what the code shows, and the fix is **not yet confirmed on the Pi**.

| Cause found in the code | Fix |
|---|---|
| espeak-ng synthesised and played at the same time, and Piper was piped straight into `aplay`. While text reading holds all four cores of a throttled board, the player runs out of samples | Every sentence is rendered completely to memory, then played. Playing a finished buffer needs almost no processor time. `aplay` gets a half-second device buffer |
| The English voice (`en_US-amy-low`) produces 16 kHz audio and the player was started at 22,050 Hz: 38 % too fast and too high | The player is started at the sample rate written in the voice's own file |
| Sound devices (HDMI, USB, Bluetooth) swallow the first syllable while they wake up | 150 ms of silence before each piece, 80 ms after (`TTS_LEAD_IN_MS`) |
| One voice process per sentence, with a gap while the next one starts | The next sentence is rendered while the current one plays; short fixed prompts (mode names, "please wait") are kept in memory and start at once |

## 2. A natural Bangla voice

The stand-alone Piper program for the Pi crashes on the public Bangla voice (`bn_BD-google-medium`), so Bangla was spoken with espeak-ng. Piper's Python package (`piper-tts`) speaks that voice without the fault. The engine now tries, per language: Piper through Python, the Piper program, espeak-ng. A voice that fails is not tried again in that run. `TTS_VOICE=espeak` forces espeak-ng.

Measured on the laptop (`speech_check.py --quiet`): loading the Bangla voice 2.9 s (done in the background at start-up), then 0.2 to 0.5 s to render sentences of 1.5 to 4.3 s. **Rendering time on the Pi is not measured.** It will be several times the laptop's, and longer while text is being read; that is the cost to weigh against the better voice, by ear, on the Pi.

Machine-listener check: six Bangla sentences from this voice were given to Google Web Speech (bn-BD). Two came back exactly, two with small differences (a numeral, a spelling), two with wrong words. This says the voice is largely intelligible to a recogniser; it is not a listening test with people.

## 3. Voice commands

Offline, Vosk small English model (68 MB), closed grammar of 39 phrases for 11 commands. A mode word selects the mode and triggers one capture. Guards: the whole utterance must be one phrase; every word at confidence 0.7 or more (fixed before the measurement, not tuned on it); the microphone is ignored while the glass speaks and for 0.4 s after; optional wake word.

Measurement with synthetic speech (one Piper voice, two Windows voices; 39 phrases each; white noise added):

| Noise | Commands | Carried out | Wrong command | Not heard | False commands on 90 English non-command sentences | on 10 Bangla sentences |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 117 | 116 (99.1 %) | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 20 dB SNR | 117 | 116 (99.1 %) | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 10 dB SNR | 117 | 113 (96.6 %) | 0 | 4 | 1 | 2 |

Sixty seconds of noise alone: no command.

**What this is not:** people, a Bangladeshi accent, the glass's microphone, a street. All of that is unmeasured. The false commands at 10 dB (a Bangla sentence heard as a command twice in ten) say that in a loud Bangla-speaking place the wake word (`VOICE_WAKE_WORD=glass`) should be on.

Command words are English because no offline Bangla recogniser of this size exists. Bangla command words are future work.
