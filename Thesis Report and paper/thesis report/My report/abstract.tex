\begin{center}
    \textbf{\LARGE Abstract}

\end{center}
\vspace{1.5cm}
\par
Visually impaired people in Bangladesh have few affordable assistive devices that work without the internet, speak Bangla, and understand local banknotes. Most commercial and research smart glasses depend on a phone or a cloud service and are built for English. This thesis presents \textit{Savior Glass}, an offline, Bangla-first assistive smart glass designed around a Raspberry Pi~5, a camera, five physical push-buttons and audio output. The glass provides three core modes: (i) Bangla and English text reading with EasyOCR, where capturing text and reading it aloud are separate button actions; (ii) continuous announcement of everyday objects with a YOLOv8 detector and Bangla class names; and (iii) Bangladeshi Taka recognition. For currency recognition we reformulated the problem as object detection and built a copy-paste compositing pipeline that pastes 5,073 background-free notes onto 1,500 real COCO photographs, generating a 22,315-image dataset with automatic bounding boxes. A fine-tuned YOLOv8s detector reaches 0.995 mAP@0.5 and 0.847 mAP@0.5:0.95 on held-out synthetic composites, and names the correct denomination for 91.5\% of 1,536 independently collected note photographs (Bangla Money), but for only 18.5\% of hand-held close-ups (NSTU-BDTAKA). To address counterfeit notes we trained a multi-view MobileNetV3-Small + TinyViT authenticator on the JaalTaka dataset (1,390 physical notes, six views each, note-disjoint split). A prefix-robust training protocol raises single-view accuracy from 73.6\% (CNN+ViT baseline) to 97.1\%, and six-view accuracy from 91.8\% to 98.1\%. The authenticator does not transfer to whole-note photographs: on genuine notes it said ``counterfeit'' for 20--59\% of them, so the glass announces the denomination and states that the counterfeit check was not done. Supporting modules include an EfficientNet-V2-S facial-expression recogniser (86.5\% accuracy on the official RAF-DB test set) for emotion-adaptive speech, Piper/espeak-ng speech with mixed Bangla--English segmentation, and haptic confirmation. The measured OCR character error rate is 3.2\% for English and 27.8\% for Bangla, showing that Bangla scene-text reading is still the weakest part of the system. We report every number from saved evaluation files. We also state plainly what has not been measured yet: Raspberry Pi~5 latency and a user study with visually impaired participants.
