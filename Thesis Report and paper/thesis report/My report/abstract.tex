\begin{center}
    \textbf{\LARGE Abstract}

\end{center}
\vspace{1.5cm}
\par
Visually impaired people in Bangladesh have few affordable assistive devices that speak Bangla, understand local banknotes and work without the internet. This thesis presents \textit{Savior Glass}, a Bangla-first assistive smart glass built on a Raspberry Pi~5 with a camera, five push-buttons and audio output. The glass has five modes: Bangla and English text reading, object announcement, Bangladeshi Taka recognition with a safe counterfeit check, facial-expression announcement, and an optional online scene description. All modes except the last run on the device. For currency recognition we built a copy-paste compositing pipeline that places 5,073 background-free notes on real photographs and labels them automatically, giving 22,333 images. A YOLOv8s detector trained on them reaches 0.995 mAP@0.5 and 0.849 mAP@0.5:0.95 on 2,282 held-out composites and names the correct denomination for 91.5\% of 1,536 independent photographs, but for only 18.5\% of hand-held close-ups. For counterfeit notes we studied the JaalTaka dataset (1,390 notes, six views each). Training on random prefixes of the views removes the collapse of multi-view models when few views are captured: one-view accuracy rises from 71.2\% to 98.2\% over three seeds. A serial-number audit then showed that the usual split lets the same counterfeit print appear in training and test. On a split with unseen prints, accuracy falls to about 90\%, and adding a check of the banknote watermark, located by a learned 4.3~MB localizer, brings it to 95.0\%, level with a fine-tuned ResNet-50. Because a wrong verdict harms a blind user, the glass never says ``counterfeit'': it says ``likely genuine'' only above a threshold fixed on validation data, and otherwise asks for a check by hand; no test counterfeit (0 of 88) was passed as genuine. For text reading we built a benchmark with correctly shaped Bangla and separate validation and test phrases and fonts. Selecting the main text block, tuned detector settings and clean-up rules reduce the character error rate from 9.3\% to 2.4\% overall and from 11.9\% to 3.2\% for Bangla. On the Raspberry Pi~5 the currency check takes a median of 327~ms and the system ran for 30~minutes without thermal throttling, but text reading takes about 19~seconds per capture. In the first hands-on test with the glass camera, currency recognition was unreliable and Bangla text was not read, so the laboratory figures do not yet describe the worn device. A user study with visually impaired participants has been prepared, with a voice-driven questionnaire tool, but has not been carried out. Every number is taken from a saved result file, and negative results are reported.
